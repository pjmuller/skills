# Official records: field-level preflight

Company filings, gazettes and registers often hold real exposure that the law requires. Don't drop them from the report (that hides a true exposure) or call them removable (that's a false positive). Classify **each field** and only then pick a remedy. Checked 2026-10-28.

## Preflight, per document

1. **Document type**: the full original act (notarial deed, signed statutes), a statutory **extract** drafted for publication, a filed annual account, a register entry, or an aggregator copy. Different rules apply to each: a field the original must contain may be optional in the published extract.
2. **Jurisdiction, entity form, date**: the law in force on the filing date *and* today; note any amendment in between. Rules differ by legal form (limited company, partnership, nonprofit) and by stage (incorporation, appointment, accounts).
3. **The person's role**: founder, shareholder, director/representative, proxy holder, auditor or liquidator. Lists differ by role. A sole founder can hold every role.
4. **Each field → mandatory / optional / unknown**, citing the provision you opened (article plus a short quote). "Identity" in a statute or an official's reply does not automatically mean every identifier (name, birth date, birthplace, home address, ID number). Find the article that lists the fields. A registered-office address that happens to be the person's home is still mandatory, as the office address. Check whether the law offers an elected or professional address option for future filings.
5. **Remedy per class** (separate routes, never promised):
   - *Mandatory*: a real exposure that is not actionable for removal. Mitigate going forward (move the registered office, elect a professional address, file future documents minimally). Erasure of lawfully required register data is generally not a right; any national limit-of-access route is case by case.
   - *Optional, or more than the law requires*: a candidate for a data-protection erasure or rectification request to the publisher, because the publisher is a controller even though it publishes "as received". Name who drafted the field (often the professional who filed it). Outcome uncertain; escalation goes to the data-protection authority.
   - *Unknown*: say so and name the one question that would settle it. Don't guess.
   - Copies elsewhere: aggregators, search indexes and archives each need their own request, and that request is stronger for fields the source didn't have to publish.
6. **Claims about immutability** ("published records can never be changed"): the original publication normally stays immutable as a record, and a correction usually comes as a new notice. Data-protection erasure of non-required data is a separate legal route; test it, don't accept a blanket answer.

## EU anchors (opened 2026-10-28)

- [C-231/22](https://curia.europa.eu/juris/liste.jsf?num=C-231/22) (11 Jan 2024): an official journal that must publish third-party documents "as they stand" can be a GDPR controller; facts: an extract held data the law did not require, the data subject requested erasure, and the national DPA ordered compliance.
- [C-200/23](https://curia.europa.eu/juris/liste.jsf?num=C-200/23) (4 Oct 2024): the register is the controller, also for non-required data; a practice of refusing every erasure of non-required data in a published constitutive act breaches Art. 17 GDPR.
- [C-398/15 Manni](https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/upload/docs/application/pdf/2017-03/cp170027en.pdf) (9 Mar 2017, press release): no right to be forgotten for legally required company-register data; after a long period following dissolution, member states *may* restrict third-party access in exceptional cases.

## Worked jurisdiction: Belgium (company code WVV, consolidated text 2026-10-28)

[WVV on Justel](https://www.ejustice.just.fgov.be/cgi_loi/change_lg.pl?language=nl&la=N&cn=2019032309&table_name=wet): art. 2:8 §2 (contents of a company incorporation extract), art. 2:14 (the extract is what gets published, the deed is filed), art. 2:12 §2 (filed documents can be inspected at the court registry; online access restricted since 8 Jan 2024), art. 2:7 §5 (elected professional domicile), art. 2:54 (directors may elect domicile at the registered office), art. 3:12 §1 1° (annual-accounts director list). Corrections: Royal Decree 29 Apr 2019 art. 1:9 §5 (a new filing marked "verbetering"; the original publication stays). The national case behind C-231/22 is DPA decision 38/2021 (erasure ordered); its final appeal outcome was not verified, so don't cite it as settled precedent. [Notarial law](https://www.ejustice.just.fgov.be/cgi_loi/change_lg.pl?language=nl&la=N&cn=1803031630&table_name=wet), art. 12: the deed itself lists the parties' birth place and date.

| Field (founder of a BV/NV) | Deed | Published extract | Annual accounts (director) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Name, first name | mandatory | mandatory (2:8 §2 4°) | mandatory (3:12 §1 1°) |
| Domicile | mandatory | mandatory (2:8 §2 4°); elected professional domicile possible (2:7 §5) | mandatory; electing the registered office is common practice |
| Birth date, birthplace | mandatory (notarial law art. 12) | **not listed** in 2:8 §2 | not listed |
| Registered-office address | mandatory | mandatory (2:8 §2 2°) | mandatory |

Fictional example: a gazette extract says "Ms A. Example, born in Sampletown on 1 Jan 1980, residing at 1 Office Lane", and 1 Office Lane is also the registered office. Name and address: mandatory exposure, mitigate going forward. Birth details: optional in the extract, so they are a candidate for an erasure request to the gazette (controller), with no promised outcome.
